# Tomato — advice bank

Each section below is one retrieval chunk. `classifier_label` is the exact
string AgriLens's classifier emits for this class — the retrieval step
matches on it directly.

---

### Tomato___Bacterial_spot
classifier_label: Tomato___Bacterial_spot

**What it looks like:** small, dark, water-soaked spots on leaves that turn
brown and may have a yellow ring; spots can also appear on stems and fruit.
Spreads fast in warm, wet, rainy weather, and by splashing water, tools, or
handling wet plants.

**General management:**
- Avoid overhead watering; water at the base of the plant.
- Remove and destroy infected leaves/plants; don't compost them.
- Don't work in the field when leaves are wet.
- Rotate out of tomato/pepper family crops for at least one season in an
  affected plot.
- Copper-based bactericides are a standard recommendation for this disease,
  but exact product and dose must come from your KVK or agriculture officer.

### Tomato___Early_blight
classifier_label: Tomato___Early_blight

**What it looks like:** dark brown spots with concentric rings (a
"target" or "bullseye" pattern), usually starting on older, lower leaves
first. Leaves yellow around the spots and drop early.

**General management:**
- Remove lower infected leaves and improve airflow around plants (spacing,
  staking).
- Mulch the soil to stop spores splashing up from the ground onto leaves.
- Avoid overhead watering.
- Fungicide use and dosing should be confirmed locally — early blight
  fungicide programs are common but product/timing varies by region.

### Tomato___Late_blight
classifier_label: Tomato___Late_blight

**What it looks like:** large, irregular, water-soaked greenish-black
patches on leaves, often with a pale green-yellow border; a white fuzzy
growth may appear on the underside of leaves in humid conditions. This
disease can destroy a crop within days in cool, wet weather — treat it as
urgent, not routine.

**General management:**
- This is the most serious common tomato disease — if suspected, contact
  your agriculture officer immediately rather than waiting.
- Remove and destroy infected plants promptly; do not compost.
- Avoid overhead irrigation and working in wet fields.
- Preventive fungicide programs exist for high-risk weather windows —
  timing and product must come from local advisory, not a generic app.

### Tomato___Leaf_Mold
classifier_label: Tomato___Leaf_Mold

**What it looks like:** pale green or yellow spots on the upper leaf
surface, with olive-green to grayish-purple fuzzy mold on the underside
directly below the spots. Favors high humidity (common in polyhouse/
greenhouse tomato).

**General management:**
- Improve ventilation and reduce humidity around plants — this is the
  single biggest lever for this disease.
- Avoid overhead watering and overcrowding.
- Remove infected leaves.

### Tomato___Septoria_leaf_spot
classifier_label: Tomato___Septoria_leaf_spot

**What it looks like:** many small, circular spots with dark borders and
gray/tan centers, usually starting on lower leaves, often with tiny black
dots visible in the center of older spots.

**General management:**
- Remove infected lower leaves and plant debris at season end (the fungus
  survives on old leaf litter).
- Avoid overhead watering; mulch to reduce soil splash.
- Rotate out of tomato for 1-2 seasons in badly affected plots.

### Tomato___Spider_mites_Two-spotted_spider_mite
classifier_label: Tomato___Spider_mites_Two-spotted_spider_mite

**What it looks like:** this is a pest, not a disease — tiny mites causing
fine yellow/bronze stippling on leaves, with fine webbing visible on the
underside of leaves or between leaves in heavy infestations. Worse in hot,
dry weather.

**General management:**
- Spray plants with water to physically knock down mite populations.
- Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides where possible — they kill the mites'
  natural predators and can make infestations worse.
- For heavy infestations, a miticide recommendation should come from your
  local agriculture officer.

### Tomato___Target_Spot
classifier_label: Tomato___Target_Spot

**What it looks like:** brown spots with concentric rings similar to early
blight, but can appear on leaves, stems, and fruit, and tends to start in
the mid-to-upper canopy rather than only lower leaves.

**General management:**
- Improve airflow and avoid leaf wetness duration (drip irrigation over
  overhead).
- Remove heavily infected leaves.
- Fungicide programs for target spot overlap with early/late blight
  programs locally — confirm with your KVK.

### Tomato___Tomato_Yellow_Leaf_Curl_Virus
classifier_label: Tomato___Tomato_Yellow_Leaf_Curl_Virus

**What it looks like:** upward curling and yellowing of leaf edges,
stunted/bushy growth, and sharply reduced fruit set. Spread by whiteflies,
not by touch or tools.

**General management:**
- This is a virus — there is no cure once a plant is infected; management
  is about controlling the whitefly vector and removing infected plants
  early, before whiteflies spread the virus further.
- Use yellow sticky traps to monitor/reduce whitefly pressure; reflective
  mulches can reduce whitefly landing.
- Remove and destroy infected plants early in the season.
- Resistant tomato varieties exist for this virus — ask your KVK which
  varieties are recommended locally.

### Tomato___Tomato_mosaic_virus
classifier_label: Tomato___Tomato_mosaic_virus

**What it looks like:** mottled light/dark green mosaic pattern on
leaves, leaf distortion/curling, stunted growth. Spreads easily by hand
contact, tools, and tobacco products (the virus is related to tobacco
mosaic virus and can survive on hands/tools for a long time).

**General management:**
- Wash hands and disinfect tools between plants, especially after handling
  tobacco products.
- Remove and destroy infected plants.
- No chemical cure exists — prevention of spread is the only lever.

---

### Tomato___healthy
classifier_label: Tomato___healthy

**What it looks like:** uniform green leaf color, no spots, no curling or
wilting, normal growth.

**General management:** No action needed. Continue standard watering,
spacing, and nutrition practices; recheck periodically, especially after
wet weather.
