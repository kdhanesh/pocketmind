### Linear equations in one variable
class: 8 | subject: Maths | topic: linear_equations
keywords: linear equation, solve for x, balance method, one variable

A linear equation in one variable is an equation where the variable (say,
x) appears only to the power 1 — no x², no x in a denominator. To solve
one, keep the equation balanced: whatever you do to one side, do to the
other.

Example: 3x + 5 = 20
Step 1 — subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15
Step 2 — divide both sides by 3: x = 5
Check: 3(5) + 5 = 20. Correct.

The core idea students get stuck on: you're not "moving a number to the
other side and flipping its sign" by magic — you're doing the same
operation to both sides, which is why the equation stays balanced.

### Rational numbers — basics
class: 8 | subject: Maths | topic: rational_numbers

A rational number is any number that can be written as p/q, where p and q
are integers and q is not zero. This includes whole numbers (5 = 5/1),
fractions (3/4), and negative numbers (-2/3).

To add or subtract rational numbers with different denominators, first
find a common denominator. Example: 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

### Quadratic equations — factoring method
class: 9 | subject: Maths | topic: quadratic_equations
keywords: quadratic, x squared, factoring, roots

A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero.
One way to solve it is factoring: rewrite the equation as a product of two
simpler expressions that equal zero.

Example: x² - 5x + 6 = 0
Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5: -2 and -3.
So: (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0, which means x = 2 or x = 3.

Not every quadratic factors neatly with whole numbers — when it doesn't,
the quadratic formula or completing the square is used instead.

### Pythagoras theorem
class: 9 | subject: Maths | topic: pythagoras
keywords: pythagoras, right triangle, hypotenuse

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the longest
side, opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other
two sides: a² + b² = c².

Example: a triangle with legs 3 and 4 has a hypotenuse of √(3² + 4²) =
√25 = 5. This is the famous "3-4-5 triangle."

### Trigonometric ratios — sine, cosine, tangent
class: 10 | subject: Maths | topic: trigonometry
keywords: sine, cosine, tangent, sin cos tan, trigonometry

In a right-angled triangle, for a chosen angle (not the right angle):
- sine (sin) = opposite side / hypotenuse
- cosine (cos) = adjacent side / hypotenuse
- tangent (tan) = opposite side / adjacent side = sin / cos

A memory aid some students use: SOH-CAH-TOA (Sine=Opposite/Hypotenuse,
Cosine=Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tangent=Opposite/Adjacent).

### Photosynthesis
class: 8 | subject: Science | topic: photosynthesis
keywords: photosynthesis, plants make food, chlorophyll

Photosynthesis is how green plants make their own food. Using sunlight,
water (absorbed by roots), and carbon dioxide (absorbed through leaf
pores), plants produce glucose (sugar, their food/energy source) and
release oxygen as a byproduct.

Simple word equation: carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose + oxygen

Chlorophyll, the green pigment in leaves, is what captures sunlight energy
to drive this process — that's why photosynthesis mainly happens in green
parts of the plant.

### Force and motion — Newton's laws (introduction)
class: 9 | subject: Science | topic: newtons_laws
keywords: newton's laws, force, motion, inertia

Newton's First Law (law of inertia): an object stays at rest, or keeps
moving at constant speed in a straight line, unless an external force acts
on it. This is why you lurch forward when a bus suddenly brakes — your
body was moving, and it resists the sudden change.

Newton's Second Law: force = mass × acceleration (F = ma). A heavier
object needs more force to achieve the same acceleration as a lighter one.

Newton's Third Law: for every action, there is an equal and opposite
reaction. When you push against a wall, the wall pushes back on you with
equal force.

### The periodic table — organizing elements
class: 10 | subject: Science | topic: periodic_table
keywords: periodic table, elements, atomic number, groups periods

The periodic table arranges all known chemical elements by increasing
atomic number (the number of protons in the atom's nucleus). Elements are
organized into rows (periods) and columns (groups).

Elements in the same group (column) tend to have similar chemical
properties, because they have the same number of electrons in their
outermost shell. For example, all elements in Group 1 (like sodium,
potassium) are highly reactive metals that form +1 ions.

### Human respiratory system
class: 10 | subject: Science | topic: respiratory_system
keywords: respiration, lungs, breathing, oxygen carbon dioxide exchange

Air enters through the nose/mouth, travels down the trachea (windpipe),
which branches into two bronchi (one per lung), then into smaller
bronchioles, ending in tiny air sacs called alveoli. Oxygen from inhaled
air passes into the blood through the thin alveoli walls, and carbon
dioxide (a waste product) passes from the blood into the alveoli to be
exhaled.

This oxygen is used by cells throughout the body for cellular respiration
— the process that releases energy from food.

### India's freedom struggle — Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)
class: 8 | subject: Social | topic: non_cooperation_movement
keywords: non-cooperation movement, gandhi, freedom struggle

Launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920, the Non-Cooperation Movement called on
Indians to withdraw cooperation from British rule: boycotting British
goods, schools, courts, and government titles, and promoting Indian-made
goods (swadeshi) instead. It was one of the first mass movements to draw
in ordinary people across the country, not just the educated elite.

Gandhi called off the movement in 1922 after the Chauri Chaura incident,
where a violent clash led to the deaths of police officers — Gandhi
insisted the movement stay strictly non-violent.

### Indian Constitution — Fundamental Rights
class: 9 | subject: Social | topic: fundamental_rights
keywords: fundamental rights, constitution, article

The Indian Constitution guarantees six Fundamental Rights to all citizens:
Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to
Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, and Right to
Constitutional Remedies (the right to approach a court if any other right
is violated).

These rights are enforceable in court — if the government or anyone else
violates them, a citizen can seek legal remedy, which is what makes them
different from other guidelines in the Constitution.

### Monsoon system in India
class: 10 | subject: Social | topic: monsoon
keywords: monsoon, southwest monsoon, northeast monsoon, rainfall india

India's climate is dominated by the monsoon — seasonal winds that reverse
direction with the seasons. The southwest monsoon (June-September) brings
most of India's annual rainfall, moving moisture-laden winds from the
Indian Ocean toward land. The northeast monsoon (October-December) brings
rain mainly to India's southeastern coast, including parts of Andhra
Pradesh and Tamil Nadu, as winds reverse and pick up moisture crossing the
Bay of Bengal.

Monsoon timing and strength directly affect Indian agriculture, since a
large share of farmland depends on monsoon rainfall rather than
irrigation.

### Globalisation — basic concept
class: 10 | subject: Social | topic: globalisation
keywords: globalisation, globalization, trade, interconnected economies

Globalisation is the increasing connection and interdependence of
countries' economies, cultures, and populations, brought about by trade,
investment, travel, and the flow of information across borders. A product
can now be designed in one country, manufactured with parts from several
others, and sold worldwide — a level of interconnection that was far more
limited a century ago.

Globalisation has both benefits (wider markets, access to cheaper goods,
spread of technology) and criticisms (job losses in some sectors from
competition, unequal benefits between rich and poor countries).
